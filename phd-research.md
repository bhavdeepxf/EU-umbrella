# PhD and Research Opportunities

## Best-fit research themes

The strongest doctoral and research matches are:

- Acoustic and ultrasonic MEMS.
- Piezoelectric materials and Aluminum Nitride (AlN).
- Thin-film deposition, ALD, PVD, and sputtering.
- MEMS fabrication and sensor integration.
- Optical and vibrational spectroscopy.
- Surface and materials characterization.
- Biomedical microsystems and smart implants.
- Medical-device engineering and translational biomedical research.

## Profile-to-project match

| Research requirement | Relevant demonstrated experience |
|---|---|
| AlN thin films | Characterization of AlN-coated 3D MEMS membranes |
| Non-planar microstructures | FTIR analysis on vertical MEMS sidewalls |
| Spectroscopy | Reflection-mode FTIR protocol development |
| Structural characterization | XRD θ–2θ scans and rocking curves |
| Morphology | SEM cross-sectional analysis |
| Stress-related analysis | Wafer-bow measurements and FTIR/XRD interpretation |
| Modelling | Fresnel-based MATLAB reflectance simulations |
| Biomedical relevance | Clinical engineering, medical equipment, imaging and OCT projects |

## Types of research organisations to monitor

- Universities with microsystems, sensor, materials, or biomedical-engineering groups.
- Fraunhofer institutes working in microsystems, manufacturing, sensors, biomedical engineering, and materials.
- Helmholtz centres with materials, imaging, medical technology, or sensor programs.
- Leibniz institutes with applied materials, photonics, and medical-technology research.
- Applied-research centres and university spin-offs developing sensors or medical devices.

## Application positioning

For doctoral applications, position the profile as a combination of:

1. Materials and thin-film characterization.
2. MEMS and acoustic-sensing research.
3. Instrument-based analytical work.
4. MATLAB-based modelling and data analysis.
5. Medical-device and clinical-engineering understanding.

This combination is particularly useful for applied research projects where device performance, materials properties, measurement methods, and clinical relevance need to be connected.
